Overview

Mind Matters is a brain-training app designed for adults aged 50 and above who want to keep their minds active and reduce the risk of memory loss or dementia. The app consists of puzzles including memory games, word games and numerical exercises — all designed to stimulate recall, critical thinking, and problem-solving.

"People who do mentally stimulating activities may have a lower risk of cognitive decline and dementia." — Alzheimer's Society, Brain Training and Dementia, December 2023

My motivation came from dementia running in my family, and witnessing the heartbreaking effects of memory loss on loved ones. The app provides visual progress charts so users can track their reaction speeds and scores over time — reinforcing consistent engagement and long-term cognitive health.

Requirements

Functional Requirements

  • Select puzzles from a main menu
  • Access game instructions before starting
  • Pause and resume timed games
  • Immediate input validation & feedback
  • Daily brain-training reminders (push notifications)
  • Toggle sound effects on/off
  • Toggle notifications on/off
  • Switch between light and dark mode
  • Record completion times and scores per session
  • Store performance history per game type
  • Weekly progress charts for engagement & scores
  • Daily challenge refreshing at midnight
  • Daily rotating brain-training quotes on home screen
  • Achievement badges per milestone (e.g. 7-day streak)

Non-Functional Requirements

  • Soothing neutral colour scheme to reduce eye strain
  • Readable font size of at least 16sp throughout
  • DataStore for user preferences (theme, sound, notifications)
  • BottomNavigationView: Home, Puzzles, Statistics, Settings
  • RecyclerView to display puzzles and instructions efficiently
  • WorkManager for scheduled daily reminder notifications
  • Room Database for authentication data, scores & statistics
  • MPAndroidChart for line, bar, and pie chart visualisations
  • SoundPool for low-latency sound effects

Technical Architecture

Room Database

Type-safe, structured data storage for game scores and performance history. Integrated with LiveData for real-time chart updates. Chosen over SharedPreferences for its complex data management capability.

BottomNavigationView

Material Design-aligned navigation with labelled icons for Home, Puzzles, Statistics, and Settings. Chosen over Navigation Drawer for direct, accessible access — especially suited to older adult users.

WorkManager

Background task scheduling for daily brain-training notifications. Persists across device restarts and ensures reliability regardless of user behaviour or device state.

MPAndroidChart

Smooth, interactive charts including line and pie charts for visualising user progress over time. Aligns with Material Design principles for a consistent and polished UI.

SoundPool

Low-latency audio for correct/incorrect answer feedback, timer countdowns, and button clicks. Chosen over MediaPlayer for its immediate responsiveness — particularly beneficial for older users needing clear sensory cues.

DataStore

Stores and manages user preferences including theme choice, sound settings, and notification preferences — providing a consistent, personalised experience across sessions.

Retrospective

Overall, I successfully implemented the majority of my initial requirements through early wireframing and a structured development approach. I expanded the app from three to six puzzles, alongside all originally planned features.

✓ What Went Well

Iterative development led to six well-crafted puzzle types rather than the planned three. Consistent planning and early wireframing kept the project on track throughout. The app was commended by my lecturer as "an excellent app that could easily go to the marketplace."

⟳ What I'd Change

Achievement badges were cut after re-evaluating relevance for older adults — comprehensive progress tracking proved more motivating. In hindsight, I'd consolidate all data under Room Database rather than splitting between Room and DataStore, simplifying data management. With more time, I'd add a cross-generational mode — family trivia or co-op puzzles with grandchildren — to support not just cognitive health but meaningful social connection.
